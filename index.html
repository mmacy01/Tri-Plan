A sprint is the friendliest way into the sport — long enough to feel like a real achievement, short enough to finish on a base you can build in three months.

Effort zones — how hard is "easy"?

Tap any workout to see its target effort. Those targets use RPE (rate of perceived exertion, 1–10):

  • Easy · RPE 2–3: fully conversational, could go all day. Most base work lives here.
  • Steady · RPE 3–4: comfortable endurance, short sentences.
  • Tempo · RPE 5–6: comfortably hard, breathing deepens.
  • Firm · RPE 7–8: hard efforts, only a few words at a time.
  • Race effort · RPE 6–7: the sustainable "strong but controlled" you'll hold on race day.

Prefer a heart-rate strap? Easy/steady ≈ zones 1–2, tempo ≈ zone 3, firm ≈ zone 4.

Transitions (the 4th sport)

  • T1 · swim→bike: goggles/cap off, helmet on and buckled before you touch the bike, shoes, go.
  • The brick workouts train your legs for that jelly feeling of running straight off the bike. Don't skip them.
  • Lay your gear out the same way every time. Race morning is not the day to improvise.

Gear checklist

  • Swim: goggles, tri suit or trunks, swim cap, wetsuit if the water's cold and it's allowed.
  • Bike: road or hybrid bike in working order, helmet (mandatory), water bottle, spare tube + inflation.
  • Run: running shoes (elastic laces save time), race belt for your number, visor or hat.
  • Both: body glide, sunscreen, a small towel for your transition spot.

Fueling & training smart

  • Keep most sessions conversational pace — you should be able to talk. Base first, speed later.
  • Hydrate before, during, and after. On longer bricks, practice sipping water and any race fuel so nothing's a surprise.
  • Eat balanced meals that leave you recovered and energized for the next session; let hunger and energy guide you.
  • Rest days are training too — that's when your body adapts and gets stronger.
  • A little soreness is normal; sharp or lingering pain is a signal to back off, not push through.

Race day

  • Arrive early, rack the bike, set up transition, walk the swim-in and bike-out so nothing's a mystery.
  • Start the swim calmer than feels natural — settle your breathing in the first 100m.
  • Ride steady, don't blow up. Save something for the run.
  • Run your own race. You trained for this. Cross the line together.

General fitness guidance for healthy beginners, not medical advice. Check with your doctor before starting — especially coming back from surgery or injury.

Optional strength workouts

Four strength sessions to fold into your week whenever it suits you — any day, no fixed schedule, totally optional. They're not part of the 12-week checklist; use them as much or as little as you like as you get deeper into training.

A good rhythm is 1–2 a week. Ideally do them on an easier day, and try not to lift heavy the day before a long brick or long run so those stay fresh. Tap any session for the full breakdown; use −/＋ to log how many times each of you has done it.

Ease into loaded movements, form over weight — and get a doctor's OK before heavy lifting if you're coming back from surgery or injury.
